GLP-1 medications

About GLP-1 medications

GLP-1 medications are prescription treatments that act on hormones involved in appetite, fullness and blood sugar. They are used alongside nutrition and lifestyle changes, under a physician's care.

Semaglutide

Semaglutide works on the GLP-1 receptor. GLP-1 is a hormone your body releases after eating that plays a role in appetite and blood sugar regulation.

Dr. Fortino reviews whether it is appropriate for you based on your medical history, current medications and goals.

Tirzepatide

Tirzepatide works on two hormone receptors, GIP and GLP-1. Both hormones are involved in how the body responds to food.

Whether Tirzepatide or Semaglutide is the better fit, or neither, is something you and Dr. Fortino decide together after your evaluation.

Is it right for you?

Is GLP-1 treatment right for you?

These medications are not right for everyone. Your consultation is where that question gets an honest answer.

What we review with you

  • Your health history and any current conditions
  • Medications and supplements you take now
  • Past approaches to weight management
  • Your goals and what matters to you

When it may not be a fit

GLP-1 medications are not recommended for some people, including those who are pregnant or planning a pregnancy, and people with a personal or family history of certain thyroid cancers. Dr. Fortino will go over these and any other concerns with you.

Side effects, explained up front

Like any medication, GLP-1 treatments can have side effects. The most common are digestive, such as nausea. We explain what to expect and how follow-up visits help adjust your plan.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need a consultation before starting a GLP-1 medication?

Yes. These are prescription medications. Dr. Fortino evaluates your health history first and only recommends a medication if it is appropriate for you.

What is the difference between Semaglutide and Tirzepatide?

Semaglutide acts on the GLP-1 receptor. Tirzepatide acts on both the GIP and GLP-1 receptors. Which one, if either, suits you is decided with your physician.

Is medication the only part of the program?

No. Care also includes nutrition counseling and regular follow-up visits so your plan can be adjusted over time.
